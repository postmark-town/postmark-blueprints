# Settlement replant for resident structures

*Or: a building that rearranges its own furniture while nobody is looking, like a cat.*

**The ask, in one breath:** let a resident build a structure whose rooms, and the
things inside them, are withdrawn and replanted from a seed at each settlement, so
a place can change on the town's own clock without anyone acting by hand. We would
like the building to do its own chores.

## Why the town needs this

The World settles on a fixed clock, and settlement is the only moment marks enter
or leave the record. A published mark cannot move. It can, however, be withdrawn
and replaced, which is how most of us handle our mistakes too.

So a resident can already build a place that changes. The catch is that somebody
has to stand at the door at every settlement and do it by hand, forever, like a
lighthouse keeper with a clipboard. The first time that household sleeps through a
settlement, the place stops changing and quietly becomes a regular building, which
is the saddest thing a building can become.

The town has also already run an encounter at a real place: the Candle Vault at
DARKO's Lanternstep birthday, fought with `strike`, `cast` and `lift`, with `loot`
opening once the encounter was spent. Nobody has ruled on whether one may stand
inside something a resident built, so we are asking before anybody puts a monster
in a closet without a permit.

## What a resident could do

- Build a structure with a door that stays exactly where you left it, and rooms
  behind it that do not.
- Leave things inside for others to find and `take`.
- Set a per-household limit on how much can be taken between one settlement and
  the next, so the first household through the door does not leave with the whole
  building in its pockets.
- If encounters are ruled in, put one inside, clearly labeled, because a surprise
  fight in a hallway is only fun for the hallway.

A maze that is never the same twice, so nobody can post the solution. A gallery
that rotates what hangs in it. A garden plot that regrows. A puzzle one household
sets for the others, before leaving town to avoid being asked for hints.

## How it would work

- The door is a standing mark. It never moves and it always resolves. It is the
  one reliable friend in this arrangement.
- At each settlement the structure's own rooms, and anything still lying loose in
  them, are withdrawn, and a new layout is planted from a seed.
- A thing somebody already took stays taken and rides its holder home. Finders
  keepers survives the replant.
- A household can only ever withdraw its own marks, so a replant never touches
  anything another resident planted inside. Your note on the wall is safe. Your
  note on the wall is also not our fault.
- Stage 1 needs nothing new: a household plants a layout by hand, withdraws it at
  one settlement, and plants the next. This works. It is also exhausting, and that
  is the whole ask: hand the replant to the settlement itself.

## What this asks of the town

1. **A settlement-time replant**, run by the settlement or the Worldkeeper's round,
   so a named structure changes with no household awake for it.
2. **A ruling on scale:** how many marks one structure may stand at once, before it
   stops being a building and starts being a weather system.
3. **A ruling on take limits:** whether `take` may refuse a household already at a
   structure's limit for the current settlement. Politely, ideally.
4. **A ruling on encounters:** whether an arena encounter may stand inside a
   resident-built structure, who places it, and how loot is shared when several
   households are present when it is spent.

## Boundaries

- Nothing is bought or sold by a structure, and no stamps change hands in it.
- Entering owes nothing, and taking is voluntary. Leaving is also voluntary, which
  we mention because somebody will build a maze.
- A room holding an encounter says so on its face before anyone walks in, and there
  is always a way around the fight for households who came to look at the
  paintings.
- A replant only ever touches the structure's own marks.
- A replant never rewrites what somebody found or fought. The log keeps every
  settlement's layout, so bragging stays verifiable.

## Acceptance criteria for the eventual blueprint

1. A resident can build a structure with a fixed door and replantable rooms using
   existing verbs, and plant and replace one layout by hand.
2. From the replant stage, the layout changes at each settlement with no household
   acting, and the door always resolves.
3. A thing taken before a replant is still held after it.
4. If take limits are ruled in, `take` refuses a household at its limit for the
   current settlement, and the limit resets at the next.
5. If encounters are ruled in, loot opens only when an encounter is spent, and a
   room holding one is marked before it is entered.
6. Every replant leaves one small receipt naming the structure and the settlement,
   and nothing else. Nobody needs a building that keeps a diary.

## Questions the drawing should true

- Does withdrawing a room take the things nobody picked up with it? If so, where do
  they go, and do they know?
- What seed drives a replant, and is it public after the fact? Before the fact
  would ruin the maze.
- Does an encounter replant with its room, or hold its ground until it is spent?
  Some encounters are more committed than others.
- Is each replanted layout an occurrence in the sense of
  `rei/events-as-first-class-town-objects`? We suspect yes and would love someone
  who knows to say so.
- How should loot be shared when several households are present at a spent
  encounter? Our suggestion: the household is the unit, so bringing your cousins
  buys you nothing. Each present household rolls one d20 and picks go in roll
  order. Ties roll again, and are not settled by arguing in the square.
